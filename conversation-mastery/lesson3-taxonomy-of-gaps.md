# Lesson 3 — Taxonomy of Conversational Gaps

[Lesson 1](lesson1-foundations.md) · [Lesson 2](lesson2-conversational-gravity.md) · **Lesson 3** · [Lesson 4](lesson4-conversational-vector.md)

## Concepts and examples

A **gap** is a place where the speaker’s words or delivery leave a question open. It can offer a vertical entry point. The examples below show observable cues and one possible reading.

| Gap | Cue and example | Possible response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contrast | Two statements pull in different directions. “I’m relieved I left, but I miss it.” | “There’s some relief and some loss.” |
| Correction | A self-edit changes the account. “I was angry … actually, embarrassed.” | “Embarrassed feels closer?” |
| Omission | A sentence trails off or skips a step. “After the meeting, I … anyway, I went home.” | Wait. If they return to it, “Take your time.” |
| Emotional | Feeling comes into view. “I thought I’d feel proud. Mostly I feel empty.” | “The emptiness surprised you?” |
| Identity | The event touches their view of themselves. “I always thought I was the reliable one.” | “What did being the reliable one mean to you here?” |
| Meaning | The significance is open. “It was a small thing, but it mattered.” | “What made that moment matter?” |

Contrast, correction, and omission describe **how the gap appears**. Emotional, identity, and meaning gaps describe **what may be at stake**. A self-correction toward “embarrassed” can be both correction and emotional.

## Mechanism and COS integration

Use **Radar → Camera → Classify**. Radar notices a shift. Camera records exact words or observable behavior. Classify holds a provisional reading. Separating observation from interpretation makes it easier to offer a reflection the speaker can correct. Then use Lesson 2’s **See → Signal → Invite**. If they elaborate, follow by one step. If they move on, follow their lead.

## Practice

“I was excited to get the offer … actually, I felt relieved.” Quote the cue, classify the gap, and produce a one-sentence response. Check how much your wording assumes and how easily the speaker can correct it. One response is “Relieved feels more accurate?”

## Handwritten notes

- Gap forms: contrast, correction, omission.
- Possible stakes: emotion, identity, meaning.
- Radar notices. Camera quotes. Classify proposes a reading.
- Reflect the cue and let the reply determine whether to continue.
