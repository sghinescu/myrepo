> The earlier curriculum outline names six directions and vertical cascades. The direction labels and practice artifacts here are provisional.

## Concepts

- **Topic** is what you’re talking about. **Vector** is the direction the speaker’s attention is taking across turns. The topic can stay “leaving a job” while the conversation moves toward emotion, a reason, or a next step.
- **Vector following** takes up the latest volunteered direction. **Vector forcing** repeatedly steers toward the listener’s preferred direction after the speaker supplies a different one.
- A **vertical cascade** is a series of speaker-led steps in depth. Each response follows the new cue. The speaker may also move laterally, pause, or return to practical details.

| Working direction | Cue within the job-change topic | Possible follow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Detail or sequence | “I gave notice on Friday.” | “What happened after you told them?” |
| Evaluation | “The handover went better than I expected.” | “What went well?” |
| Emotion | “I felt relieved as soon as I left.” | “When did you first notice the relief?” |
| Motivation | “I needed predictable evenings.” | “What made that a priority then?” |
| Meaning or identity | “I finally felt like I could make my own choices.” | “What did having that choice mean to you?” |
| Action or future | “Now I’m figuring out what to do next.” | “What options are you considering?” |

## Mechanism and COS integration

Use Lesson 3’s **Radar → Camera → Classify** on each new turn. Quote the cue, infer the current vector, and use Lesson 2’s steadiness to give the speaker room. Apply Lesson 1’s **Match → Then Lead (+1)** within the direction they opened. Reassess after the reply. The six directions are working categories. People can move among them in any order.

## Example and practice

> “I changed jobs.” → “How’s the new role going?”
>
> “I’m relieved. I used to feel like I had to prove myself every day.” → “What’s different about that feeling now?”
>
> “I feel like I can be a beginner again.” → “What does being able to start fresh give you?”
>
> “Right now I’m just trying to get through onboarding.” → “How’s onboarding been so far?”

The conversation moves from events to felt experience and self-understanding, then returns to practical details. For the drill, identify each cue and generate a different response to the last turn that fits its practical direction.

## Handwritten notes

- Topic = subject. Vector = where attention is moving.
- Quote the latest cue. Follow it with one step and read the reply.
- A cascade consists of volunteered steps. Lateral movement is part of the river.
